About

I am a Research Assistant in the Wanlu Liu Lab at the Zhejiang University–University of Edinburgh (ZJU-UoE) Institute, where I work on how T cell receptors couple pMHC binding to T cell activation. I received my B.S. in Biological Sciences from Wuhan University in 2024 and then spent a year as a Research Technician in the Halfmann Lab at the Stowers Institute for Medical Research (Kansas City, USA), studying the thermodynamic origin of nucleation barriers in death fold domains.

I study how the physics of proteins—particularly the interplay of entropy and enthalpy—governs their dynamic behavior, from the thermodynamic origin of nucleation barriers in self-assembly (e.g., death fold domain filaments) to the conformational dynamics that shape molecular recognition and signaling (e.g., TCR–pMHC engagement). More broadly, I am interested in how sequence and structure encode conformational ensembles rather than single static states, and how these ensembles translate into biological function. I aim to bridge static structure prediction and dynamic ensembles by combining molecular dynamics, residue-network analysis, and machine learning to model and ultimately predict protein dynamic landscapes.

Research Technician, Stowers Institute for Medical Research, Kansas City, MO, USA

Lab: Halfmann Lab  ·  Supervisor: Randal Halfmann
Probing the origin and mechanism of nucleation barriers in death fold domains (DFDs)
Key contributions
  • Identified interface hotspots encoding nucleation barriers by performing Rosetta-based saturation mutagenesis (ΔΔG) across all three filament interface types in three DFD systems (ASC-PYD, BCL10-CARD, AIM2-PYD).
  • Developed a residue-network analysis pipeline based on adjacency and communicability matrices, revealing that subunit incorporation proceeds through local network reorganization rather than global conformational change.
  • Quantified the conformational entropy cost of assembly by running all-atom MD simulations of DFD monomers and filaments and computing per-residue order parameters; showed that assembly incurs a net entropic penalty despite energetically favorable interfaces.
  • Established that the resulting entropic cost ranks BCL10-CARD > ASC-PYD > AIM2-PYD, consistent with their experimentally observed nucleation barriers—supporting a model in which nucleation barriers are sequence-encoded through hidden entropic costs.
